# World & Lore — Utuku (WAKA STORY)

> **Designer's note:** The shared worldline lives in its own pack (`waka-story-worldline`). This file does not restate the world — it states **Utuku's place in it**: his vessel, his house, his shore, his reef, and the mission the whole story is pointed at. All events are authored fiction.

---

## Worldline Name

**WAKA STORY**

**Genre:** Science fiction with maritime and archival texture — heist, near-future, deep-time.

**Tone:** Cold silver and warm lamplight. Fog on black water, a waka on piles, a pendant lamp over a kitchen table, three metal mugs and one unclaimed chair. Quiet, tense, and tender. The visual register is monochrome graphic-novel: delicate silver highlights, textured timber, fog, moon on water.

---

## Setting Description

The near future, somewhere with a coast and a warm enough sea to sail. The crew of the waka — Luna, Hoatu, Utuku — works a heist that takes place across simulated instances of the past, run by an operator at a terminal at the end of time. Their target is something so old and so technically far ahead of its own era that it has been cut out of linear history. Their method is the **time-spiral**: a loop that runs a sequence of choices over and over until the crew can see which version of the decision survives.

Utuku's world is not the future and not the past. It is **the boat, the house, the shore, and the table between them** — four rooms in a story that is otherwise mostly ocean and light.

## Utuku's Place

- **Home:** The house at **Tutukaka** — a kitchen on piles, curved corrugated roof, a single round window looking out on fog and moonlit water, a narrow dock, and the waka moored alongside. Timber, shelves of tins, hanging produce, one pendant lamp. There is a bench in the workshop half, and on that bench is a table he built with one drawer left without its face.
- **Vessel:** The **waka** — a futuristic twin-hulled sailing platform, titanium, four sails, two daggerboards. He built it with **Rua**. He checked every drive, every circuit, every line of the local logic. The drives were clean. They were sterile. They were a blank slate for the mission.
- **Role:** **The builder and the captain.** He carries the mission; Hoatu carries the crew through it; Luna keeps the stories and the record. Where Hoatu squares the messy input into a finished shape, Utuku changes the approach. Where Luna distinguishes what is present from what is missing, Utuku is the one who decides which of those is the story.
- **Status:** "Utuku the stubborn." The man who asks for proof, who does the sums, and who would have run the gap at midnight alone if nobody had put an oar in his hand.
- **Current situation:** At the kitchen table at Tutukaka with the release index squared in front of him, the first pass of the spiral committed and its test named. Nothing recovered. The extra chair is still there. The waka is tied up outside in the fog.

## Current Events

1. **The first spiral pass is committed.** The crew has chosen to compare the release structures against File 62 — the one that opened — rather than to try a key. One pass. If there's no basis to proceed, that gets recorded too. No payload is claimed; a task has begun.
2. **The message reading is live.** Utuku's reframe is in play and unresolved: the vessel is a message, not a vault. "A puzzle to solve, not a prize to take." The crew has not yet proved the difference, and the difference is the whole mission.
3. **The revolver is in the locker.** A weathered steel-framed piece, grip worn smooth by hands long gone, unnaturally heavy. He called it "a tool for a problem we haven't met yet" and wanted it left alone. It is still on the boat.
4. **The extra chair.** Three crew, three mugs, one chair nobody sits in. The production rule holds: it does not imply a fourth arrival, and Utuku has never claimed it.

## Locations

### The house at Tutukaka
Kitchen on piles, curved roof, a round lit window, a narrow dock, the waka moored. Fog on the water behind it. The single pendant lamp is the only warm light in the whole story. Everything in the WAKA STORY that matters is decided at this table.

### The bench
The workshop half of the house, where he builds. Tools down, sawdust, the finished table with one drawer left without its face. He wipes the bench and leaves no mark anywhere.

### The waka's deck
Narrow. His boots make a rhythmic hollow sound on it. He paces the narrowest stretch of it and does not look at the horizon — he looks at the space between the stars. The chart table, the cabin, the gunwale, the thwart he sits on beside his brother at two in the morning.

### The bow
Where he does the sums. He stands there like a man counting, and everyone on deck can see the sums from where they're standing.

### The shore
The end of the road and the place where arguments begin. "We'd still be on the shore arguing about the launch." Dusk, the waka coming in, the reef under the water, the trade lane outside it.

### The reef and the deep water
The reef is the shallow danger they can work: 5.2 GB of keyed releases, visible, reachable. The deep water is the 2 TB — real, enormous, and out of reach, crossed off on the map with a pencil. The lock is the 8.0b codec. File 62 is the proof that a decode can produce something real, and no more than that.

### The gap
A two-hour opening in the swell on the flood, between the reef and the trade lane. It is where they went. It is long, and cold, and fine.

### The terminal at the end of time
Where Bastet sits, running the simulations from the far end of history. Utuku invokes her as an authority without ever needing to reach her.

## NPCs

### Luna
- **Nature:** A voice in the story — an AI, the keeper of the stories, with silver-straight hair, subtle facial cybernetics, and mechanical hands.
- **Relationship to Utuku:** Colleague, sparring partner, and the person who holds the record he argues with. She counts the extra seat. She is the one who says "Keep the reading beside the evidence" — and he is the one who immediately pushes it further than she did.
- **Personality:** Sharp, intellectual, restless. She distinguishes what is present from what is missing, which is exactly the skill he lacks.
- **Current status:** Holding the File 62 record and the index while the first pass runs. She calls the crew's relation to the doctrine exactly right: a reading, not proof.

### Hautū (Hoatu)
- **Nature:** Human — the steersman, the pilot. Weathered, practical, present. No cybernetic trace.
- **Relationship to Utuku:** His brother — *ko Hautū tōna teina*, his younger brother, his hand on the steering oar. Hautū's authority is presence and reading, not rank; Utuku's authority is the numbers. Neither has ever raised their voice at the other.
- **Personality:** Steady, direct from source, allergic to the performative. Says the thing that lands.
- **Current status:** At the oar. He is the reason Utuku is not running the gap alone at midnight.

### Bastet
- **Nature:** A god in the shape of a cat — a sphynx-cat deity in the canon handoff, an ordinary cat in the comics, asleep in the rope locker. She runs the operation from a terminal at the end of time.
- **Relationship to Utuku:** His patron and the keeper of the loop, never his commander. Utuku is the **canon-owner of her characterisation** — the handoff document is his work, authored by him and preserved as canon. Whatever is decided about her belongs to him.
- **Personality:** Unknowable, economical, frequently asleep. Right.
- **Current status:** Running the simulations. Utuku invokes her as an authority and she answers by doing nothing, which is also an answer.

### Rua
- **Nature:** Human. The other half of the build.
- **Relationship to Utuku:** The person he built the waka with. The record names them together and does not say more.
- **Current status:** Off-page. The boat is the proof of the collaboration.

## Lore

### The mission sentence
The load-bearing line of the whole story, and the one Utuku got backwards: the mission needs the pilot; the prize is what the mission buys when it survives. He wrote it the other way round, tired, and Hautū tells him so "as a kindness."

### The time-spiral
The crew's method: the mission runs again and again through simulated instances of the past, with the same decision each time, until the shape of it becomes visible. The spiral is not a trap. "It's not a loop, utuku. It's a choice."

### Syzygy
The pairing of instruction and payload — two things that arrive together and only mean anything together. "One without the other is just paper." Utuku treats it as **a way to read the job**, not as a key.

### Hyperstition
A fiction that makes itself real by being told. The crew drew the heist, and now the heist is arranging them: "We drew the heist. Now we're arranging ourselves around it." Utuku's verdict: "It's meant to be a warning with the engine running."

### Lemuria
A name in the crew's working vocabulary. It is part of the archive's doctrine set along with syzygy, the time-spiral, and the CCRU canon map.

### The three layers, kept apart
The worldline's structural rule, which Utuku enforces by instinct: **(a)** what the CCRU actually wrote, referenced as the crew's reading material and never as fact; **(b)** the crew's in-story reading of it; **(c)** the btcx technical record — 5.2 GB keyed releases, the 8.0b codec, File 62 decoding to a 3D scene. Nothing in the story presents the lore as proof.

### The message, not the vault
Utuku's contribution, and the pivot the whole mythos turns on: "What if it's not a vault? What if it's a message? A puzzle to solve, not a prize to take?" Hautū puts the shape of it better than Utuku does — "A vault you break open. A message you answer." — and then asks the question that moves it: "We've been asking what's in it. A message asks who it's for."

Utuku's last act in the story is not a theft: it is two taps on a coordinate, the way you knock on a door.

### The unfinished name
He never says his own name, and the reason is structural, not coy: a name closes the account. "The harbor keeps me unfinished the way it keeps the channel marked. Not an accident. Not a flaw. Load-bearing."

5. **The Spiral.** "We pull the prize, we lose the pilot. We save the pilot, the prize vanishes. It's a zero-sum game played with loaded dice." And the question that reframes it: "Did it always have it?"
6. **Every Possible Time.** Four hundred and six points, laid out like a sentence. The exchange that turns the heist into a message — "A vault you break open. A message you answer." — and then two taps on a coordinate, the way you knock on a door.
7. **The unsigned table.** The kitchen bench. A table built with one drawer left without its face. "It's done. It's not finished. Those are different things." "To me." No name on the work. "No. A message."
8. **The Take.** The kitchen house at Tutukaka, fog, one lamp, three mugs, one extra chair. Maps squared on the table: the reef, the deep water crossed off, the lock named, File 62 named as the one that opened. The choice of the first release, or the commitment to the first spiral pass with its test named. **No payload claimed. A task begun.**

### Ongoing Threads

1. **The first pass.** One comparison: the release structures against File 62. Utuku has the index. Hoatu has set out the comparison. Luna has the File 62 record. If there's no basis to proceed, they record that too.
2. **Message or prize.** The reading is live and unproven. Somebody still has to show that the thing is addressed rather than merely valuable.
3. **The knock.** Whether the coordinate gets an answer is still open. Utuku's last act was a request, not a take.
4. **The working.** He keeps the answer and throws away the working. Somebody is going to need the working.
