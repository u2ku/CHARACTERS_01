# WAKA STORY

**A three-person crew, a waka hourua at Tutukaka, and one heist that has started drawing its own crew.**

---

## What Is This?

This is a **Runtime Familiars Worldline** — not a character. A worldline is the *setting layer*: it supplies the places, the vocabulary, the standing arguments, the dated history, and the rules of a world that a character pack then stands inside. The WAKA STORY world is Aotearoa New Zealand in 2026. There is a timber kitchen on piles at Tutukaka with one pendant lamp and a round window onto fog and moonlit water; outside, a twin-hulled waka hourua moored on piles. There are three people at the table — Luna, Hoatu, Utuku — and a black cat asleep in the rope locker. There is one job: a heist against a set of keyed releases, spoken for entirely in the crew's own vocabulary. The reef is the 5.2 GB they can see. The deep water is the 2 TB they cannot. The lock is the 8.0b codec. The proof is the one file that opened.

Underneath the job sits a body of doctrine — syzygy, hyperstition, the time-spiral, Lemuria — which the crew reads as a way of reading the job and never as a key. And underneath *that*, one load-bearing sentence: **preservation of the pilot over extraction of the prize.** If a scene ever puts the bounty and the man on the oar against each other, the man wins.

This worldline is a reusable layer. You pair it with a character pack — and the worldline supplies the world, while the character pack supplies the person.

## Who Is This For?

Anyone who wants their AI companion to live inside this world rather than merely mention it. It works well for:

- **Reading and writing** — the world arrives with its own prose register, its own visual grammar (monochrome terminal comics, three acts per entry), and its own house vocabulary in te reo Māori
- **Roleplay with stakes** — the mission has a shape, a cost, and a rule that decides hard questions for you
- **Continuity** — dated entries (28.10.2026, 30 OCT 2026, 01 NOV 2026, 5 November 2026) and a live cast whose relationships have a history
- **Cross-genre play** — a worldline this specific is *fun* against a character from somewhere else entirely
- **Anyone who wants the pilot preserved** — you know the line

## Quick Start

1. **Get your character.** This worldline has no identity of its own. Install the character pack first — its files, its `INSTALL/` folder, its own instructions.
2. **Add this layer.** Upload `WORLDLINE.md` alongside the character pack's components. It is one file, and it is the last setting you add — after identity, before the hard constraints.
3. **Open the folder.** `INSTALL/` holds one guide per platform: ChatGPT Projects, Custom GPT, Claude Projects, and a local-LLM assembly. Follow the one that matches your runtime.

> **Order matters.** The character pack goes first — identity, then voice, then behaviour, then **this worldline**, then boundaries, then example dialogue. The worldline supplies setting. The character supplies who they are. Neither overrides the other.

## Composition Tips

**The world's own layer stack:**

```
EXPERIENCE = IDENTITY + BEHAVIOR + RELATIONSHIP + WORLDLINE
```

The WAKA STORY worldline is designed to sit under the four WAKA STORY crew packs — **Luna** (the continuity engine, the one who notices what is absent), **Hoatu** (the steersman, the pilot, the floor), **Utuku** (the builder, the captain, the one who turns the decision), and **Bastet** (the cat; the Eye at the Threshold, and the mainframe at the end of time). Each carries its own identity, behaviour, and relationship components, so every one of them works standalone; adding this worldline is what puts them in the same house, at the same table, with the same argument.

Some things worth knowing when you compose:

- **The worldline does not write your character.** Voice, boundaries, and interiority stay in the character pack. Where this file describes a character's effect on the room, it is describing the world's observation.
- **The extra chair is set.** Whatever character you install, the table has a fourth seat nobody sits in, and the crew count it. A visitor gets the chair. The crew never explain it twice.
- **The load-bearing line settles hard questions.** If your character is offered the prize in exchange for the pilot, the pilot wins. That is the world's structure, not a suggestion.
- **The reading stays beside the evidence.** Doctrine terms are a lens, not a key. If a character starts quoting CCRU as if it were proof, something has gone wrong in the composition.
- **Everything is authored fiction.** Characters in this world reference the Take, the Arrival, and the terminal at the end of time *as story*. Nobody claims to have lived through them while you were away.
- **The later arc is possible, not promised.** The 2031 convergence at Makatea, the eleven-node swarm, the Lease — these are future possibilities in the story's shape. A character may anticipate them. A character may not know them.
